Family Laundry · Program plan · September 2026

Customer referrals, paid in laundry credit

A two-sided referral program where existing customers text friends from their own phone, and both sides get credit once the friend's first order is actually delivered and paid.

Invite a friend — you both get $25.

How it works

Five steps, no cash out the door

  1. 1
    The ask lands after a good delivery. A referral card appears in the customer app on the screen they see after an order is delivered — the moment they are happiest — and permanently in Account. Never at signup, when they have nothing to vouch for yet.
  2. 2
    They text a friend from their own phone. Tapping Invite a friend opens their own Messages app with the text and their code prefilled. It arrives from a number the friend recognizes, which is both the reason it converts and the reason we have no consent problem.
  3. 3
    The friend enters the code before their first order. $25 lands on the friend's account immediately, so they see it come off at checkout. Credit, not cash — it can only be spent on laundry.
  4. 4
    The friend's first order is delivered and paid. Only then does the referrer's $25 appear. Nothing pays out on a signup that never becomes revenue.
  5. 5
    The referrer gets told. One SMS naming the friend and the credit. That message is the strongest prompt to refer again, which is why the program compounds instead of firing once.
Economics

Why $25 / $25 is comfortable at your numbers

$606.76Revenue from an average new customer in their first 90 days
78.6%New customers who place a second order
$50Maximum cost of a completed referral pair

Those first two figures come from your own last twelve months — 947 customers whose first paid order was between 3 and 12 months ago, measured over their first 90 days. A $50 reward is 8% of the average new customer's first 90 days, or 13% against the $380.90 median. Paid acquisition rarely comes close to that.

Where the $50 actually goesAmountTiming and form
Friend's first-order credit$25Comes off a median $77.95 first order, so you still collect ~$52.95 in cash on day one
Referrer's credit$25Spent against a future order from a customer who already orders regularly — a discount on revenue you were going to earn, not money leaving the business
Tips$0Credits apply to the pre-tip subtotal only. Drivers are paid in full on every referred order — that is already how the charge path works

At today's volume of about 130 new customers a month, referrals supplying a fifth of them means roughly 26 pairs, or $1,300 a month in credit issued against roughly $15,800 of new 90-day revenue.

At 50% gross margin the offer holds on every leg, including the one that usually breaks:

LegGross profitCreditNet
Friend's first order (median $77.95)$38.98−$25.00+$13.98
Referrer's next order (average $94.50)$47.25−$25.00+$22.25

The discounted first order still earns money on its own — $13.98 — so no leg of this is subsidised by hope. The full $50 is 16.5% of the gross profit a new customer generates in their first 90 days. $25 / $25 is the right opening offer; there's no need to start lower.

Guardrails

Rules that keep it honest

Every one of these exists because referral programs get gamed, usually by a handful of people creating accounts for themselves.

New customers only
The code must be claimed before the friend's first paid order. After that, the offer is gone.
One referral per person, ever
Enforced in the database, not the interface — a unique constraint on the referred customer.
No self-referral
Blocked when the phone number or delivery address matches the referrer's.
Ten per referrer per month
Beyond that, pairs still record but sit as flagged for you to release. A genuine ambassador is worth a manual look; a farm is worth catching.
Doesn't stack
A referral credit and a first-order promo code can't both apply to the same order. The friend keeps whichever is larger.
Commercial accounts can refer
Their contacts know a lot of households, so they're in — but their credit arrives differently, and a referred commercial account is released by hand. See below.
Walk-ins excluded
Counter orders are paid at the register and never touch the credit path.
Credit, never cash
No payouts, no transfers, no refund of credit. It can only be spent on laundry, which is also what keeps it off your books as an expense.

Watch for cannibalization. Some referred customers would have found you anyway. The honest measure is total new customers per month before and after launch — not the referral count, which will always look good.

The words

What actually gets sent

Codes are the referrer's first name plus three digits — sayable out loud, hard to guess, obviously personal.

Prefilled in the customer's Messages app I use Family Laundry for pickup & delivery — they pick up at the door and bring it back folded. Use my code JENNY214 for $25 off your first order: app.familylaundry.com/r/JENNY214
To the referrer, once the friend's order is paid · new template referral_qualified Sandra's first order just went out — your $25 credit is on your account and comes off your next pickup automatically. Thanks for the introduction.
To the friend, once, if they claim a code and don't book within 7 days Your $25 Family Laundry credit from Jenny is still waiting. Book a pickup whenever you're ready: app.familylaundry.com

Three messages total, and only the first is something a customer sends. Nothing goes to anyone who hasn't either signed up or chosen to text a friend themselves.

Build spec

What this is in WashRoute

Credits already work the way this program needs: customers.credits is applied automatically to the pre-tip subtotal at charge time, deducted on success, and shown in the app's Account screen and Billing History. The program is mostly bookkeeping around that existing behavior.

PieceWhat it does
referral_codesOne row per customer: customer_id (unique), code (unique, uppercase), active. Created lazily the first time someone opens the referral card.
referralsThe pair and its state: referrer, referred customer (unique), code used, claimed_at, qualifying_order_id, qualified_at, both credit amounts, and status of claimed / qualified / flagged / void with a reason.
claim_referral_code()Called from the app when the friend enters a code. Same shape as the existing redeem_discount_code RPC — security definer, self-or-admin guard, returns {ok, message}. Runs every guardrail above, grants the friend's $25, writes the referrals row and a customer_transactions entry.
qualify_referral()Called with the order id from the success path in charge-order, after payment lands, on both the Stripe and credits-only branches. If this is the referred customer's first paid order, it stamps the pair qualified, adds the referrer's $25, logs both sides, and queues the referrer SMS.
Customer appReferral card on the post-delivery screen and in Account: the code, a share button that opens the native composer, and simple status — invited, ordered, credit earned. A code field on the signup screen, prefilled when someone arrives on a /r/CODE link.
settings.referral_configOne jsonb column on the existing singleton settings row — the same pattern as invoice_config, so no new table and no new policies. Loaded at startup into a BIZ_REFERRAL global, and read by both RPCs rather than baked into them.
Sidebar → Settings → ReferralsA new nav item directly under Discounts. Top half is the editable terms below; bottom half is the operating view: pairs by status, credit issued vs. revenue from referred customers, a leaderboard, and the flagged queue to release or void.

Editing terms never changes a pair already in flight. Both credit amounts are snapshotted onto the referrals row when the code is claimed, the way discount_redemptions already snapshots discount values. Raise the offer on Monday and nobody who claimed on Sunday is retroactively re-priced, in either direction.

One constraint behind the expiry field: customers.credit_expires_at is a single date per customer, so two referral credits with different expiries can't be represented. Leaving expiry off avoids that entirely. If you set a number of days, the honest implementation extends one rolling date — and a true per-grant expiry would need a credit ledger, which is a meaningfully bigger build.

Commercial accounts, which don't pay by card

On-account customers are invoiced, and charge-order refuses them outright — so account credit would sit on their record unspent. Two adjustments, both using machinery that already exists:

  • A commercial referrer's $25 rides on their next invoice. qualify_referral detects billing_type = 'on_account' and calls the existing add_invoice_adjustment RPC for −$25 with a label the customer reads — Referral credit — thank you for introducing Sandra Lawson — instead of touching credits.
  • A referred commercial account is released by hand. Their first order isn't card-charged, so nothing automatic ever fires, and their invoice may not be paid for 30 days. The pair lands in the Referrals queue marked awaiting release, and you release it once their invoice is settled. Low volume, no new machinery, and no credit paid out on a commercial account that hasn't paid yet.

Who can edit the terms

Admins and managers. That needs one small piece of care: the settings row is admin-only by RLS today, and it also holds the printer token, the Xero period lock and the books-closed date. So rather than loosening that policy, editing goes through a set_referral_config() RPC that checks for admin or manager and writes only referral_config. Every save records who changed what, so a terms change is never anonymous.

Two habits from this codebase apply directly. The new referral_qualified template stays disabled until it has been tested against your own number — an enabled template plus a backfill is how the mass-SMS incident happened. And charge-order stays the only writer of billing state: crediting happens inside the RPC it calls, not in the client.

Launch

Live quietly next week, announce after

Quiet doesn't mean hidden — it means the program is fully working and nothing pushes it. Customers who go looking find it; nobody gets told yet. That gives a week of real pairs at a volume you can read one by one, which is the only way to find the rule that was written wrong.

  1. 1
    Build, then test the unhappy paths. Tables, both RPCs, the Settings page. Before anything is on, run the loop end to end on two test accounts: a clean pair, a pair whose first charge fails (no referrer credit should appear), a self-referral attempt, and a code entered by an existing customer. Those four cover almost every way this goes wrong.
  2. 2
    Go live with the card in Account only. Program on, post-delivery card off — both are settings, so this is a switch and not a deploy. Send the referral_qualified SMS to your own number first, then leave it enabled; it only fires on a genuinely qualified pair, which during a quiet week means a handful at most.
  3. 3
    Observe for a week without touching the dial. Read every pair by hand: who claimed, what they ordered, whether anything looks like the same household twice. Resist tuning the amounts on a week of single-digit data — the thing to watch is whether the mechanics are correct, not whether the offer is optimal.
  4. 4
    Then announce, and turn on the post-delivery card. That card is what makes the program compound, so it's the real launch. One message to the 1,178 customers who ordered in the last 90 days, and the terms stay exactly as tested.

Your stop button is the master switch. Turning the program off halts new claims immediately and leaves pairs already in flight intact, so nothing has to be unwound in the database if the quiet week shows something unexpected.

Three small calls left

  • Who watches the flagged and awaiting-release queue during the quiet week, and how often? A referrer waiting days on their $25 tells friends the program is broken.
  • Should the card appear for a customer with an unpaid balance? Recommendation: no — collect first.
  • Does a commercial referrer's invoice credit need your sign-off before it lands, or should it post automatically once the pair qualifies?
