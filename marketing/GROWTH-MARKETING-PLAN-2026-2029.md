To grow 30% a year we must stop the customer treadmill first (fix signup drop-off, churn and win-backs — mostly free), then restart paid local marketing at about $8–9K a month with strict cost-per-customer rules, and add commercial accounts and new territories in years 2–3.

## Where we are

Revenue is about $190K a month (~$2.3M a year) and slipping: order revenue fell from $192K in May to $159K in September. Active delivery customers have been flat at ~660 since May.

## The target

30% a year for 3 years means 2.2x today's revenue: about $420K a month by September 2029. At ~$270 a month per active customer, that's roughly 1,460 active customers (or fewer, if commercial accounts carry part of it).

| By | Annual run rate | Monthly revenue | Active customers needed* | Net gain per month needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Today (Sep 2026) | $2.3M | $192K | ~660 | — |
| Year 1 (Sep 2027) | $3.0M | $250K | ~870 | +18 |
| Year 2 (Sep 2028) | $3.9M | $325K | ~1,130 | +22 |
| Year 3 (Sep 2029) | $5.05M | $420K | ~1,460 | +28 |

*Residential-equivalent at $270/mo. Every $2,700/mo of new commercial business replaces ~10 residential customers.

Today net gain is about zero. Year 1 needs +18 net customers every month: that's the whole game.

## Where the growth comes from

Year 1 needs +$58K a month. The seven levers below add up to ~$90K, so the plan still hits target if a third of it underdelivers. The first four cost little and come first.

| # | Lever | Year-1 goal | Added revenue/mo by Sep 2027 | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Turn signups into first orders | 55% → 70% of signups order | ~$13K | Low (automated texts/emails + first-order offer) |
| 2 | Cut churn | ~95 → ~80 customers lost a month | ~$16K | Low (save offers, recurring push) |
| 3 | Win back dormant customers | Reactivate ~60 of ~5,500 dormant accounts for good | ~$16K | Low (credits) |
| 4 | Referrals | 1 → 15+ referred first orders a month | ~$11K | $50 per new customer ($25 + $25 credit) |
| 5 | Paid local marketing | ~35 first orders a month from ads/mail | ~$22K | ~$6–7K/mo |
| 6 | Commercial accounts | 5–6 new school/daycare accounts at ~$2–3K/mo | ~$12K | Sales time, some commission |
| 7 | Subscription upsell | +40 subscribers ($275/mo) | Retention boost, counted in #2 | Low |

The $59 → $65 price rise likely hurt trial. Keep the price; lower the risk of the first order instead (e.g., $20 off first order, satisfaction guarantee).

## The plan by phase

Fix the leaks before buying traffic: every paid customer is wasted if 45% of signups never order and churn cancels the rest.

### Phase 1 — Fix the leaks (Oct–Dec 2026, ~$2–3K/mo)

- **Attribution first.** Make "How did you hear about us?" required at signup. Give each channel its own promo code and tagged link. Without this, no channel can be judged.
- **Signup-to-order nudges.** Automatic text + email at 1 hour, 1 day, 3 days and 7 days after signup with a first-order offer and the next open pickup window.
- **Make it recurring.** After the 2nd order, prompt customers to set a recurring pickup. Recurring is already the largest revenue source.
- **Churn early-warning.** Flag customers who skip two cycles, rate 3 stars or lower, or open a CS issue. Send a save offer; ask why when they stop.
- **Win-back campaign.** Segment the ~5,500 dormant accounts (lapsed since March vs. old Starchup-era) and send a "we miss you" credit in batches of ~500. Run WashRoute preflight before every send.
- **Announce referrals.** Post-delivery card, a text to every active customer, a bag-tag insert. Goal: 15 referred first orders a month by March.
- **Reviews.** Send the Google review link to every rater (never only happy ones). Goal: double Google reviews in 6 months. No Yelp review asks.
- **Google Business Profile.** Weekly photos/posts, correct hours (pickup Mon–Sat, counter 7 days), service-area zip codes.

### Phase 2 — Test paid channels (Jan–Jun 2027, ~$6–9K/mo)

- Google Local Services Ads (pay per lead) and Google Search ads for "laundry delivery Oakland/Alameda".
- Direct mail and door hangers on streets already on our routes (94610, 94501, 94611, 94601, 94602). Denser routes = cheaper deliveries.
- Restart Yelp at a lower cap, tracked with its own code. Keep only if it meets the cost rule below.
- Nextdoor neighborhood ads and local sponsorships (schools, sports teams, community events).
- Commercial outreach focused on schools and daycares, our best sector so far: licensed childcare centers in Alameda County (linens, nap-mat covers, bibs) and school districts and charters in Oakland, Alameda, San Leandro and Berkeley (athletics uniforms, PE, nurse linens), plus school and city RFQs. Time pitches to the school calendar (budgets in spring, start in August). Target 2 new accounts a quarter.

### Phase 3 — Scale what works (Jul–Dec 2027, ~$9–12K/mo)

- Move budget from losing channels to the 2–3 that meet the cost rule.
- Add a part-time B2B salesperson on commission once commercial proves out.
- Seasonal pushes: back-to-school, holidays, spring cleaning (comforters, bedding).

### Years 2–3 — Expand the footprint

- **New areas via Uber Direct.** Test demand in adjacent cities (e.g., Berkeley, Emeryville, San Leandro, Castro Valley) before adding our own routes.
- **Commercial as a second engine.** Aim for commercial at 20–25% of revenue by year 3.
- **Buy customer lists.** Approach retiring laundromat or wash-and-fold owners to buy their route/customer base. Often cheaper than ads.
- **Raise revenue per customer.** Add-ons (bedding, hang-dry, dry-cleaning partner) and subscription tiers.
- **Capacity.** Line up plant capacity with the building purchase so growth isn't capped by production.

### Focus segment: seniors

Seniors are a growing share of our customers and a strong fit: they value pickup and delivery most, tend to stay loyal, and respond to mail and word of mouth.

- **Senior communities as routes.** Partner with independent-living and 55+ communities, senior centers and home-care agencies. A set building pickup day gives dense, cheap stops.
- **Mail that speaks to them.** A senior version of the route mailer: large type, "we carry the bags," simple ways to book.
- **Adult children as buyers.** Offer "gift a month of laundry" and a pay-for-a-parent option; promote it around holidays.
- **Booking without the app.** Text booking (the planned AI text booking) and recurring pickups by default.
- **Measure it.** WashRoute has no age data today, so add an optional "Are you booking for a parent or relative?" question and tag senior-community accounts to confirm the trend.

Year 1 spend is about $85–95K (~3.5% of revenue), ramping from $2–3K a month now to ~$9K by spring. Nothing scales until it meets the cost rule.

| Commercial sales (commission/materials) | — | 500 | 1,500 |
| **Total** | **~2,500** | **~8,750** | **~11,250** |

**Cost rule:** a channel stays only if it brings a first order for $150 or less. At ~50% gross margin, a new customer's ~$600 in first-3-month revenue is ~$300 of gross profit, so $150 pays back in about 6 weeks. Hard ceiling $200. Review every channel monthly; cut after 2 months above $150.

## Constraints

Capacity is the first wall: the plant can handle ~20% more orders with today's team, which is ~$230K a month. The plan reaches that around spring 2027, so hiring must start before then.

| Constraint | What it means for the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Gross margin ~50% | $150 cost per new customer pays back in ~6 weeks; $200 is the hard ceiling |
| Plant capacity +20% with current team | Headroom runs out at ~790 active customers (~$230K/mo), likely Mar–Jun 2027. Start hiring/adding a shift when daily orders reach +15% over today (Q1 2027) |
| David owns marketing | Automate Phase 1 inside WashRoute so it runs without daily effort. In Phase 2, add a freelance ads manager (~$500–1,000/mo) so paid channels don't depend on your time |
| Best commercial sector: schools and daycares | Commercial lever targets them first; other sectors (short-term rentals, gyms, salons) only after 2 wins |
