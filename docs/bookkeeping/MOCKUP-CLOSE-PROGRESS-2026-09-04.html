The close bar — four ways to show where August stands

Replacing the white “Not ready to close” strip on the Loans page with something that answers how far along are we, not just no.

Counts below are today’s real book (14 active loans — 12 close on lender statements, 2 on a contractual schedule), shaped the way _bkStatementGate() computes them. The live component would read that function, never its own arithmetic.

Option A

One segmented bar — the statement gate, made visible

The bar is the gate: every required statement is a slice, coloured by how far it got. Green is uploaded and checked against Xero, amber is on file but never compared, red is still owed. The two schedule-basis loans sit outside the denominator and are named, so the denominator can’t quietly shrink.

Option B

Three questions, three bars — statements, schedules, books

Your three asks as three separate meters, because they fail independently and get fixed by different people: a missing document is an upload, an unchecked one is a reconciliation run, a loan off its schedule is a staging problem. Each bar names what’s missing right underneath it rather than making the reader scan the table.

Option C

A milestone track — where the month is in the pipeline

The close is a sequence, and this says which stage it’s stuck at rather than how many items are outstanding. The most “fun” of the four and the one that reads fastest from across the room; the trade is that it shows the shape of the problem, not the size of it — so the named detail has to live in the line underneath.

Option D

One line — bar, verdict, and the ask

The smallest thing that could work: keeps the strip a single line, adds a slim bar for the glance, and spends the remaining width on what to actually do. Nothing new appears on the screen except eight pixels of colour.

What I’d pick

B, with D’s discipline. You asked for three specific numbers and B is the only one that keeps them separable — a month can be 12/12 on statements and still stuck because two loans fell off their schedules, and A collapses that into one bar that can’t show it.
  • Every bar that isn’t full names its loans — that’s the standing rule that a blocking chip answers its own question, so nobody scans fourteen rows to reconstruct a list the code already has.
  • Denominators never shrink quietly. The two schedule-basis loans are excluded from the statement bars by recorded policy and counted in a sentence, not dropped.
  • The verdict word stays. The bar is the progress; “Not ready to close” is still the claim, and it stays computed from every gate — not from whether the bars look full.
  • Green means checked, never merely uploaded. A document on file with no tie-out is amber. That distinction is the whole point of the gate.
If B reads too heavy once it’s on the real page, D is the fallback and it degrades to exactly the strip you have now.
